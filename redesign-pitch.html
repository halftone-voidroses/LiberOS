READ ME FIRST The scope covenant

The rule that governs every pitch below, written once so it is never argued per-room.

The bar is the midway.

The Games midway — camera-aware booths, per-attraction material design, posters, sound, save flow — is the minimum quality for every room in this document. A pitch's scope contract is not a stretch goal list; it is the definition of done. An agent that ships half a contract has not shipped the room.

Contracts bind three ways.

Every scope contract lists (1) must-ship deliverables — the concrete artifacts the build produces; (2) acceptance checks — how the build is verified in a browser, not in prose; (3) explicit non-goals — what may be cut if time runs out, and nothing else. Anything not listed as a non-goal is in scope.

Impressive means exercised.

Every contract closes with a Playwright pass through its acceptance checks on the real served app, and screenshots compared against the mocks in this document. Design that cannot survive a browser at 1280×800 and 439px is not done.

SYSTEM 01 Liberchat — the talking machine

One lamp on every page. Every conversation in the machine runs through it — and every persona is built to full depth. There is no legacy chat surface.

IThe Lamp with a Chatbox

A small phosphor lamp persists on every page. Open it: a real chatbox. You type, the room's traveller answers in full voice, remembers the conversation within the visit, and knows what you just did in their room. The old cohort and buddy chat pages retire; their personas live here, rebuilt to the same advanced standard as every other traveller. Nothing is "kept around" — the machine has one conversational surface and it is modern everywhere.

  • Persona engine: per-traveller script in data/personas.data.js extended with conversation state — greeting, topic memory, context hooks (last action in this room), hesitation lines, unlock barks. Deterministic + seeded first; reply interface abstracted so a model-backed responder can slot in later without UI change.
  • Conversation-driven affinity: exchanges feed the existing affinity thresholds alongside metrics, so booths and deep features unlock by being vouched for.
  • The buddy's sealing flow lives in the lamp: any conversation can be sealed as an artifact with the wax animation.
  • Presence rules: 26px silent lamp when closed; speaks only when spoken to or when the room owes a beat. aria-live="polite", full keyboard path, motion-gated.
scope contract · must ship
  • Lamp component injected on all 17 pages; chatbox with persistent per-visit history per persona
  • Full conversation scripts for all ten+ travellers — no persona ships with fewer than 40 written lines across greeting/context/hesitation/unlock registers
  • Buddy and cohort personas rebuilt here; buddy.html chat and cohort.html retired to archive notes pointing at the lamp
  • Affinity wiring: exchanges count toward thresholds; at least one locked booth demonstrably unlockable by conversation alone
  • Seal-to-artifact from any conversation; Enter-to-send; Escape closes; reduced-motion respected
  • Acceptance: Playwright conversation through 3 personas, unlock triggered by chat, seal persisted in state
cost ~6 days · lamp + persona engine + scripts + affinity wiring + retirements

SYSTEM 04 The Evolving Constellation

The desktop is not a launcher. The icons are the state: the buddy and every artifact grow more complex as relations are set, and they change the moment you bind — not on your next visit.

IIcons That Remember

hefty · the desktop grows
born
1 keep
3 relations
7+ · crowned
the altar grows what you bind · it changes the moment you bind it

Every icon on the desktop becomes a procedural render of your state, drawn in layers: base form → material → earned complexity. Nothing is a fixed PNG; the same data always draws the same icon, so the icon is the data, legible at a glance.

  • The buddy stone is born smooth. Each kept artifact type carves its own mark into it — a dart-wheel for games, a wave for the sea, a star-spread for tarot, a leaf for the garden, a thread for dreams. Relations cut facets into the stone (conic planes that catch light); the patina tiers deepen the carving and warm the tint. A heavily-relationed stone looks like a different object than a new one, because it is.
  • Artifact glyphs accrue orbits. One ring per relation; satellites for two-way or annotated relations; inner facets for notes. Set a relation and both endpoints morph in place — one earned animation beat, the edge drawing itself between them, no reload. Motion-gated like everything else.
  • Edges mature with their relationship: a fresh relation is rope, an annotated one a brace, a long-lived one a glowing filament. The whole constellation precesses — slow rotation per patina tier — so a lived-in desktop visibly turns.
  • Release is not erasure, even optically: remove a relation and the ring retracts, leaving a faint ghost line on the stone — the icon remembers its former complexity, exactly as the Trash covenant promises.
scope contract · must ship
  • Procedural icon renderer for the buddy stone and all artifact glyphs, layered from real state (artifact types, relation counts, note presence, patina tier) — zero fixed image assets
  • At least five distinct stone carve-marks by artifact type; facets from relation count; tint/deepening per patina tier
  • Live morph: setting a relation updates both icons in place with one motion-gated beat and an edge draw; unbinding retracts the ring and leaves a ghost line
  • Three edge textures (rope → brace → filament) and patina-tier precession; reduced-motion gets static tiers
  • Deterministic rendering (same state → same pixels); legible and non-broken at 439px
  • Acceptance: Playwright keeps an artifact → stone gains its mark; binds a relation → both icons morph live with rings; unbinds → ghost line; screenshots of stages 0–3 differ; constellation still fully operable at 439px
cost ~4.5 days · icon renderer + relation/patina hooks on desktop; state and relations already exist
why the desktop and not another room — the report already names the constellation the product's moat: "memory you can see." This pitch cashes that in. Every other system feeds it: Liberchat seals feed the stone, the Powder Sink keeps feed marks, the Glasshouse tree hangs fruit in it, the room behind the CRT shelves its volumes. The desktop is where all of it becomes visible at once — and with live morphs, responsive at the exact moment the visitor acts.

ROOM 08 Toybox

A full powder game in a sink, with a fully designed crew of little dudes who live in it.

IThe Powder Sink, fully simulated

hefty · the sandbox grows up
tap · matchbox · salt · seeds · wall · erase — and the crew

Full powder-game simulation — the classic falling-sand rule set, running per-particle in the tray: gravity piling and slipping, water spreading and leveling, fire consuming and rising as steam, oil floating and burning dirty, salt dissolving, seed sprouting in wet sand, wall for building, erase for undoing. Elements react: water + fire → steam, fire + oil → sustained burn, salt + water → brine, seed + damp sand → sprout, steam condenses back to water on cool. Brush size, pour rate, and a clear/drain control — the toy that ships must be a real sandbox someone could play for an hour, not a canned animation.

The crew — fully designed little dudes who walk the sim and treat it as world, not backdrop:

  • The crab (flagship): a proper sprite with a sidestep walk cycle, digs into sand piles, sidles around water, flees fire, waves a claw when you pour near it. It has opinions and Liberchat voices them.
  • The snail: trails a slow slime line across stone and wall, shelters in its shell when fire is near, rides floating oil rafts.
  • The duck: bobs on any water surface, paddles to spilled seed, shakes off when lifted out.
  • More residents unlock with the machine (affinity-gated): each new creature is a full design pass — walk cycle, sim interactions, one Liberchat register — never a recolor.
scope contract · must ship
  • Particle sim at ≥60fps for a full tray at 1280×800 (spatial hash; pause/drain/clear), all eight base elements + four named reactions
  • Diegetic dispensers (tap, matchbox, salt cellar, seed packet) as the element selectors — large hit targets, keyboard operable
  • Crab, snail, duck shipped as full designs: sprite sheets or drawn canvas frames with walk/swim/flee states and concrete sim interactions each
  • Keep-the-picture flow (snapshot of tray + crew) into desktop + satchel, unchanged save semantics
  • Acceptance: pour water on fire → steam rises; seed on wet sand → sprout; crab flees fire; 60fps sustained sim; Playwright covers each reaction
cost ~8 days · sim engine + creatures + dispensers; the single largest room build in the pitch

The room: a full glasshouse — panes with condensation the cursor wipes, a watering can with a visible arc, roots visible in a glass side-panel, seedling bed, time-of-day light, motion-gated.

The tree — the idle game, promoted: the thimble garden's mechanics reborn as a small tree in its own pot. Watering (can, or ask Ruby through Liberchat) speeds the current stage. The tree also appears in the window box of the room behind the CRT — the two systems see the same plant.

  • Thimble booth migration: existing thimble state feeds the tree's starting growth; no kept data lost
  • Ruby's Liberchat register: stage comments, harvest celebration, watering on request
  • Tree mirrored in the room-behind-the-CRT window box
  • Acceptance: plant → tree persists across reload with correct offline growth; harvest grants rewards; Playwright simulates time jump; reduced-motion static growth states all distinct

CLOSING Order of work — the whole contract

1. Split the games monolith.

Every flagship sits on it. No exceptions, no "temporarily add to games.js."

2. Liberchat, complete to its scope contract.

Lamp, engine, ten+ full personas, buddy + cohort retirement, affinity wiring. The machine learns to talk; everything after uses its voice.

3. The Powder Sink and the Glasshouse Tree.

The two biggest room builds — the simulation and the idle engine — with their contracts as definition of done. Either one alone is a release-quality headline.

4. The Room Behind the CRT, then Rainy Day.

The memory palace and the storm, wired together, both bound by contract.

5. The Evolving Constellation.

Procedural icons, live morphs, ghost lines — the renderer and its hooks, contract-bound. The desktop is the face of every other system; it should age as they do.

6. Rooms in descending visit order.

Sigil's stone-and-company, the Felt Table, Dreams fog-to-dusk, the Promenade, then the quiet rooms — each closing its own contract with a Playwright acceptance pass before the next begins.

The covenant, restated once more:

The midway is the floor. Every contract above is the definition of done for its room. Half a contract is not a shipped room — it is a corner cut, and corners are the one thing this document exists to prevent.

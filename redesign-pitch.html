liber.os · redesign pitch · visual approaches per room

One machine, twelve rooms,
twelve chances to be a place.

The last release made the rooms behave. This pitch makes them belong. Every room below gets two or three hefty visual directions — full-surface treatments, not skin jobs — each mocked here in CSS so the approach can be judged as design before a line of it is built. Each is written against the covenant: no shared stylesheets across rooms, personality through material, motion respects the visitor.

ROOM 01 Casting Stone — sigil

Hero action: intention → stroke → save. The stone is the hub of the whole OS; its room should feel like the workshop where the machine's heart was cut.

IThe Chisel & the Vein

bold
physius · the cutting bench
Treat the drawing surface as literal stone: a chiseled rim cast in real shadow, grain that catches the cursor light, and — the hefty part — carved veins that glow faintly copper where the visitor has drawn, as if the mark were cut into rock rather than inked on it. Undo becomes "the vein cools." Save becomes "set the stone." Physius speaks in chisel metaphors; the dust from cutting gathers in a tray under the canvas.
cost ~3 days · new canvas layer + grain shader-in-CSS; reuses save flow

IIThe Dark Atelier

quiet
physius · atelier
a single candle. the stone waits on the bench.
One candle of warm light on a near-black bench; the stone sits in the pool of it and everything else recedes. Tools appear only when the cursor nears the bench edge — a workshop, not a toolbar. The room hums at low frequency; the candle gutters when the visitor hesitates. Quieter than I, but the most atmospheric room in the machine.
cost ~2 days · mostly CSS lighting; existing tools restyled in place

ROOM 02 Chat — buddy

Hero action: speak to the part, two exchanges, seal. The room must make a deterministic bot feel like a presence without pretending to be a person.

IThe Seance Lamp

bold
i feel stuck
stuck is a place. tell me the walls.
the lamp keeps the circle · answers arrive in their own time
The conversation happens inside a circle of lamplight on the floor — messages as objects in the circle, the buddy's replies arriving with a slow lamp-sway that makes waiting feel intentional. Replies are unmistakably theirs: wax-seal avatar, their material's texture on the bubble edge. Sealing the transcript becomes lowering the lamp: the room dims, the circle closes, the wax cools. Late replies get a physical excuse ("the lamp considered it") instead of a spinner.
cost ~3 days · reply-pending state already exists; new visual layer

IICorrespondence

quiet
let me write that down properly.
Letters, not bubbles. Each exchange is a small folio card that slides into view; the buddy's handwriting (a display font) differs from the visitor's typed line. The transcript "seals" into an envelope that visibly joins the constellation. Turns the room from "chat app" into "the part that writes back" — slower, more literary, more in keeping with the Satchel.
cost ~2.5 days · transcript rendering restyle; seal flow unchanged

ROOM 03 Cohort — the older forge

Hero action: confess, exchange, seal. Deliberately kept as the continuity room; the pitch is dignity, not deprecation.

IThe Wax Archive

quiet
e-lizabeth · the older forge
compact exchange, honest wax
Black wax, sealed flame-flicker, a tape-recorder honesty: the room frames itself as the archive desk where the older ritual is kept working. Transcripts scroll like tape logs; the seal is a wax stamp with real depth. A small brass plate reads "the older voice is kept here on purpose." Addresses the plan's ask: label continuity without shame.

ROOM 04 Divination

Hero action: ask → draw / cast. Structure speaks; the room should feel like a table where the unexpected is welcome.

IThe Felt Table

quiet
arcana · chalk rim · deep red felt
Commit to the séance-parlour card table: deep red felt with a real chalk-dusted rim (reversible chalk marks for the question), cards that slide from a physical deck with weight and settle misaligned like real cards do. The I Ching cast shares the same table: coins become chalk circles on the felt. The room stops feeling like two disconnected tools and starts being one table with two games.
cost ~3 days · unifies Tarot + I Ching presentation (also closes a report P1)

IIThe Observatory

risky
Lift the room into an orrery: hexagram lines drawn as constellations in a domed dark, tarot spreads as planetary arrangements. Genuinely striking, and the most "haunted CRT" of the options — but it competes with the desktop constellation (the product's crown jewel) and drifts from the tactile table the copy promises. Worth it only if the desktop keeps its own scale.
cost ~4 days · new visual language for both draws; watch constellation collision

ROOM 05 Dreams

Hero action: record before it thins. Writing here is catching something dissolving; the room should make haste feel safe.

IThe Ledger of Fog

bold
the dreamer · ledger of fog
write it while it is still wet —
The input is a page that is still drying: a faint fog creeps at the screen edges and the page's margins mist over as the visitor types — the room visibly helps them catch the dream before it thins. The reading arrives as marginalia in a second hand. Associations become threads stitched between saved dreams in the ledger view.
cost ~3 days · fog layer + marginalia restyle; flows unchanged

IIThe Dusk Nursery

risky
Dreams as a shelf of dusk-polaroids: each kept dream develops slowly (blur → focus) and gains a kept image; the ledger is the shelf. Beautiful, but it visualizes outputs more than the act of recording, and the recording is the hero action. Better as a future Satchel view than as this room's spine.
cost ~3.5 days · ledger + shelf views; copy tone shifts slightly

ROOM 06 Sea

Hero action: name → weigh → release. Stillness is the feature; the room must never dramatize the visitor's burden.

IThe Tide Clock

bold
vanir · the tide decides the pacing
A brass tide clock governs the room: releases happen on the water's own schedule, the waterline visibly rises a hair with each kept release, and the room's stillness has a readable mechanism. Vanir's deep-intensity unlock becomes a deeper water tone. The observation ask from the report ("release completion observable without breaking stillness") is answered by the clock's single slow hand, not by confetti.
cost ~2.5 days · tide pacing exists; adds clock + waterline state visual

IIBlack Water, Small Light

quiet
Near-total dark. The visitor's line of text is the only lit thing — a small paper boat that takes the words and drifts out of the light. No meters, no clocks, nothing to read but the release itself. The most disciplined option; risks feeling like "nothing happened" to a first-time visitor, which the tutorial would have to carry.
cost ~1.5 days · least work; needs one tutorial beat to explain the boat

ROOM 07 Games — the midway

Hero action: play one booth, keep the picture. Now nine booths including the Quiet Floor; the midway is the site's showcase surface, so this is the largest single spend.

IThe Full Promenade

hefty · flagship
step right up · nine attractions · no score, keep the picture
Promote the camera from a sliding strip to a walkable promenade: parallax ground, string lights that sag between booths, the visitor's shadow moving with the pan, per-booth barkers that call out only when their booth is centered. The featured Quiet Floor gets the cold-lit floor plate it already has, and entering any booth keeps its stage curtain animation. This is the version the pitch deck screenshots are made from.
cost ~5 days · camera layer rework; booth logic untouched (the monolith split should land first)

IIRainy-Season Midway

bold
the tents are up. the rain does the drumming.
Same midway, off-season: rain streaks on the "lens," bulbs reflected in wet ground, banners dulled, the barkers quieter — and the machines glow warmer for it. Emotionally deeper than I (the fair kept running anyway) and cheaper, but it trades reach for mood and makes the room less legible as a first stop.
cost ~3 days · weather layer + palette shift; camera untouched

ROOM 08 Toybox

bold

ROOM 09 Garden

SYSTEM-WIDE Three moves that bind the rooms together

These are the approaches that make twelve rooms feel like one machine without sharing a stylesheet.

ALiberchat, the companion lamp

hefty · new feature
the room speaks
one line, in the voice of wherever you are
The plan's dormant centerpiece, pitched properly: a small phosphor lamp that persists across every page and speaks in the current room's traveller's voice — Wanderlust on the desktop, Vanir at the sea, the Scribe in Learn. Not a chatbot: one line of context, a hint when the visitor stalls, silent the rest of the time. Persona data already exists in data/personas.data.js; status-line retires into it.
cost ~4 days · one injected component + persona lines; deprecates status-line.js

BThe Patina System

quiet
One state curve — patina-1..3 from visits + artifacts (already computed in shadow.js) — expressed as material in every room: the stone's grain deepens, the book's spine thickens, the graveyard greens, dust density rises. Rooms opt in with their own CSS; the system owns nothing but the class names. Absence changes nothing; presence accumulates. This is the return-reward the gamification plan wanted, with zero numeric UI.
cost ~2.5 days across rooms · state exists; per-room CSS opt-in

CTime-of-Day Machine

risky
The room's lighting answers the hour: candle-amber evenings, grey pre-dawn, full phosphor noon. Powerful atmosphere — and the riskiest item on the page, because it changes the perceived brightness of every surface and multiplies visual QA. If pursued, ship as a settings default with an off switch, never forced.
cost ~3 days + QA multiplier · CSS variables per hour-band; opt-out required

CLOSING Order of work, if the whole pitch is bought

1. Structure before spectacle.

Split the games monolith first — the Promenade and every future booth sit on it. Then the shared shell's confirm-key adoption completes the interaction contract.

2. One flagship room at full depth.

The Satchel Reading Desk or the Games Promenade — pick one, ship it completely, and let it set the quality bar the other rooms are renovated toward.

3. Liberchat, then patina.

The companion lamp is the biggest new capability; patina is the cheapest system-wide win. Both bind the rooms without touching their stylesheets.

4. Rooms in descending visit order.

Sigil and Buddy first (every session passes through them), Divination/Games next, utility rooms last. Time-of-day last of all, and only if the QA budget is real.

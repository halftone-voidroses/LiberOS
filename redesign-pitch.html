SYSTEM 01 Liberchat — the talking machine

One lamp on every page. The traveller of whatever room you are in answers — and conversation is not decoration: it is how the machine opens itself.

IThe Lamp with a Chatbox

hefty · flagship
whimsy wow · games
step RIGHT up. you have the look of someone about to throw a dart at their own feelings.
which booth should i even start with?
the wheel, obviously. tell it grief and pick the color. i'll know if you're honest.
say something to whimsy…enter ⏎

The pitch: a small phosphor lamp persists on every page, bottom corner. Open it and it is a real chatbox — you type, the room's traveller answers. Not a ticker, not a hint bubble: a conversation with the part who lives in this room.

  • Chatbot with depth, not a random line machine. Each traveller carries a persona script in their own voice (Wanderlust mythic, Riason dry, Vanir tidal, Whimsy barker-loud, Arcana ceremonial). Replies mix: persona flavor, context awareness (what you just did in this room), and gentle questions back. Deterministic engine + seeded prompts first; the surface is built so a model-backed reply can slot in later without UI changes.
  • The buddy folds in here. The current buddy chat room's conversation, history, and sealing become a Liberchat conversation with the buddy-stone persona — available from any page, sealable from the lamp itself. One intuitive place to talk; no second chat UI to learn. The buddy keeps its lamplit circle identity inside the lamp window.
  • Talking unlocks. This is the big one: affinity grows through conversation, not only metrics. Trade three honest exchanges with Whimsy and the next booth's tarp drops; ask Vanir about the deep enough times and the tidepool opens. The existing affinity thresholds stay — conversation depth becomes a first-class metric feeding them, so unlocking feels like being vouched for rather than grinding a counter.
  • Presence without nagging. Closed, the lamp is 26px and silent. It speaks only when spoken to, or when the room has something truly owed (a completed keep, a hesitation — see sigil). aria-live="polite", fully keyboard-driven, motion-gated.
cost ~5 days · lamp component + persona script layer + affinity wiring; retires status-line.js and absorbs buddy chat
why this shape wins — you asked for chatbot capabilities, traveller-specific rooms, and conversation-driven unlocks in one feature. One lamp, one chatbox, personas per room, and the affinity system feeding unlocks delivers all three without a second chat surface to maintain.

SYSTEM 02 The Room Behind the CRT

The screen shows the OS. Behind it is a room — and the room is where the machine keeps what you did. It fills, deepens, and ages as you work.

IThe Dark Room That Fills

hefty · the memory palace
behind the screen · the room keeps what you keep

The pitch: a place the OS never shows you on-screen — the physical room the CRT sits in, seen from behind/side like a stage you were never meant to watch. It is diegetic meta-progress: the PC itself never changes, but the room around it accumulates you.

  • A bookshelf that populates. Every satchel keep slides a ledger volume onto the shelf — spines thicken with artifact counts, ribbon markers appear for unreads, and the shelf visibly sags a little as it fills. Browsing the shelf is browsing the book.
  • A pool of shadow fluid that rises. Every sea release, trash burial, and discarded draft feeds a dark pool on the floor — the room literally holds what you put down. It creeps up a stone lip over months. Vanir's deep-unlock is reflected in its tone.
  • A candle that burns with your sessions. Session time melts one candle down; a fresh one is lit on return. Absence changes nothing — the wax is time spent, not time missed. The patina tiers (visits + artifacts) drive dust, wear on the floorboards, and the age of the furniture.
  • Room hooks per system, no new UI: tarot keeps land in a small card box on the desk; relations pin a corkboard with red string; games prizes collect as tin trophies on the CRT's top; the garden seedlings appear in a window box behind the monitor. Each hook is a small CSS/JS surface fed by state that already exists.
  • How you see it: a "look behind" affordance on the desktop (the CRT tilts, or the bezel's shadow parts) plus a settings toggle. It is also the natural home for Rainy Day — the rain falls in the room.
cost ~6 days · one new scene + per-system hook CSS fed by existing state; patina tiers already computed in shadow.js
why this exists — you wanted the system to change with time and patina to update the desktop, and to flesh out the space around the promenade. The room behind the CRT is the single place where all of it lands: every room's output has a physical resting place, the machine visibly ages with use, and the PC stays untouched.

SYSTEM 03 Rainy Day — the shadow layer becomes a place

The settings toggle becomes a desktop icon: one switch that weathers the whole machine for shadow work.

IOne Icon, One Storm

bold
☂rainy day
shadow mode · for the heavy visits
the machine dims itself for you. everything still works. nothing expires.

The pitch: a desktop icon — "rainy day" — that toggles the existing shadow-on layer into a full machine weather-state. Not a theme with different colors: a mode for heavy visits, where every room keeps working but presents its shadow-work face.

  • Per-app evocative skins in each room's own stylesheet (covenant holds): the sea goes black-water with rain circles; the games midway runs its rainy-season palette — dull banners, bulbs doubled in wet ground, barkers hushed, machines glowing warmer; the garden grows by lightning-flash; the satchel's desk lamp narrows to a reading cone.
  • Copied softer, not darker-for-darkness's-sake: confirmations slow down, timers stretch, the machine asks "is tonight a keeping night or a releasing one?" once, and stops. Hard-nights helplines surface one click closer in this mode.
  • It weathers the room behind the CRT — rain on the window the machine sits beside, the shadow pool rising faster because you are releasing more. The two systems are one picture.
  • Exit is as easy as entry — the same icon, now shining. The machine never asks why you came, and never makes you explain leaving.
cost ~4 days · icon + shadow.css deepening + per-room rainy skins; toggle/state already exist

ROOM 08 Toybox

The kitchen sink works because it can hold everything the machine ever was — including the games it retired.

IEverything But — the Kitchen Sink

bold · expanded
tap · matchbox · salt · seeds · the ball · the box — everything but

Frame the sandbox as a kitchen-sink tray under a warm bulb, and make the metaphor carry old-game-style toys — diegetic dispensers where toolbar buttons used to be:

  • Working dispensers for the sim materials: a tap that actually pours the water, a matchbox you strike for fire, a salt cellar you shake, seed packets you tear. Every dispenser doubles as a large hit target (the accessibility ask, solved by furniture).
  • Toy-box classics as new matter: a fortune-teller ball (ask, shake, one of Arcana's one-liners rises in the liquid), a music box that plays the soundscape's melody and leaves visual ripples, a wind-up diver that swims the water you poured. Small physics toys, each a kept-picture moment like the booths.
  • The sink holds the machine's past: retired tools and legacy booth mechanics return as toys — the earliest wheel becomes a spinning top, old prototypes become bath toys. "Everything but the kitchen sink" becomes literal: the machine's history is in the tray, and Liberchat's Whimsy will tell you what each toy used to be.
cost ~4 days · dispenser skins + 3 toy mini-sims; core sand/water sim untouched

CLOSING Order of work, whole pitch bought

1. Split the games monolith.

Every flagship sits on it: the Promenade cannot be built on a 1,400-line coordinator, and future booths keep paying the monolith tax until it is done.

2. Liberchat, complete.

Lamp + chatbox + persona scripts + affinity wiring + the buddy and cohort fold-ins. This is the machine learning to talk; everything after uses its voice.

3. The Room Behind the CRT.

Shelf, pool, candle, hooks — fed by state that already exists. Then Rainy Day, which weathers the same room. The two together turn "system changes with time" from an idea into a place.

4. Rooms in descending visit order.

Sigil's stone-and-company, then the Felt Table, then Dreams fog-to-dusk, Kitchen Sink, Glasshouse, Reading Desk, and the quiet rooms. Flagship-quality one at a time; the promenade renovation lands last and best.

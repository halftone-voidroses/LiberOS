LIBER · VACUI

== fate appreciates your patronage ==

a haunted computer
in a dark room.

Liber.OS is an offline art-therapy machine. You cast a cohort — a drawn figure for the part of you that waits — and you keep it company with cards, hexagrams, confessions, and releases, until what was buried starts answering back.

the premise

the room is a mind.

Imagine your mind is a village, and the people in it populate your dreams. Among its many houses there is one that is special — the house that keeps the Liber Vacui. To a blacksmith it appears as a forge. To an artist, a studio. What the machine looks like depends on who is looking; what it does depends on what they are willing to meet.

Many travellers have used it before you. Each built one tool, each left a carving on the bezel. Some of the tools are reflective. Some are divination. One of them is a tutorial traveller who was trapped in the Vacui for trying to dissect it in bad faith — you will meet him, and he will be sarcastic about the user experience.

the thesis

three pillars, one bridge.

Concepts going back thousands of years — the Tao, the I Ching, the disciplined imagination of the hermeticists — are largely missed in a colonial setting, where they survive as exotic ornament or not at all. Liber.OS tries to build the bridge differently: through esoterica, fairy tale, fiction, and archetype — with everything research supports kept research-backed, and everything invented marked as invention.

pataphysics

the method

Jarry's science of imaginary solutions — the permission to treat the imaginary as real, and to build a small universe on purpose.

eastern traditions

the cosmology

The I Ching and the dialectic of yin and yang — opposites as complementary forces in balance, not enemies to be defeated.

depth psychology

the map

Jung's archetypes, the shadow, and individuation — grounded in the working tools of DBT and the practice of art therapy.

At the centre of all of it is the Noble Shadow: the repressed parts of you that were actually beneficial. The art teacher who said you couldn't draw, so you never drew again — the drawing did nothing wrong. Shadow work here is not a fight. It is a homecoming.

how it works

a relationship, kept deliberately.

  1. i.

    cast the cohort

    Write the intention. Draw what the elements pull from your hand. What you save anchors the centre of the desktop — and there is only ever one.

  2. ii.

    make artifacts

    Draw tarot cards. Cast hexagrams. Play the booths, confess to the wax, release what you carry to the deep. Each act leaves an artifact.

  3. iii.

    declare relations

    Bind each artifact to your cohort with your own verb: tower → protects → cohort. Relations accumulate into a constellation — a map of what holds.

  4. iv.

    the machine answers

    Emergent prompts composed offline from your own materials, on a schedule no dashboard would approve of. Write, draw, or refuse. The machine does not nag. It waits.

who lives here

the cast.

wanderlust

the summoned

Pink circle, yellow circle, a single blinking white eye. She has been called Fate, Chance, Destiny, the Wheel, Samsara — and she colour-flickers through every one of those names when she says them.

raison

the trapped tutor

Dry, snarky, obsessed with good UI. He is trapped in the Vacui for trying to dismember and dissect it in bad faith. He explains the mechanics because somebody has to, and he hates this kind of thing.

mistress physius

the first traveller

Pious, hermetic, patient. She established the casting tradition — the stone tablet, the five elements, the intention written once. Her carving is the first on the bezel.

what it stands on

grounded, where the ground exists.

Everything research supports stays research-backed. The machine itself cites: the archivist traveller keeps the bibliography, and anything the author invented is presented as thesis, not finding.

Linehan, M. M. (1993). Cognitive-Behavioral Treatment of Borderline Personality Disorder. — the source of DBT and the Wise Mind construct.

Jung, C. G. (2009). The Red Book: Liber Novus. — active imagination as the model for cohort dialogue.

Tedeschi, R. G., & Calhoun, L. G. (1996). The Posttraumatic Growth Inventory. Journal of Traumatic Stress, 9(3). — the workbook's frame: growth, not repair.

Porges, S. W. (2011). The Polyvagal Theory. — why the grounding and body-mapping exercises work the way they do.

Luhrmann, T. M., et al. (2015). Hearing voices in different cultures. British Journal of Psychiatry, 206(1). — why a heard cohort is a cultural technology, not a delusion.

Henrich, J., et al. (2010). The weirdest people in the world? Behavioral and Brain Sciences, 33(2-3). — the colonial gap this project exists to bridge.

Huxley, A. (1945). The Perennial Philosophy. — the perennial thread, kept in view.

Jarry, A. (1911). Exploits and Opinions of Dr. Faustroll, Pataphysician. — the science of imaginary solutions.

safety. Shadow work is gated by intensity, never ambush. The dark mode is earned, explicit, and always has a visible way back. Agency over protection is covenant law.

privacy. Everything runs locally, from one folder. No account, no network, no telemetry. Your artifacts never leave this room.

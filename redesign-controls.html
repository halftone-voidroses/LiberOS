liber vacui · chrome pitch · rev. c · 2026-09-14

Six fittings for the machine's hand — legible chrome, made of the room

The sweep found the same three failures in every room's furniture: controls too small to hit, ink too faint to read, and shapes too generic to belong to whoever wired them. This is a pitch for the machine's chrome — every control a fitting, cut in the room's own material, carrying a word instead of a glyph, and never smaller or fainter than a floor the whole machine can be held to. Nothing here is a component library: anatomy is shared the way a door is a door; the making stays the room's.

§1The floor — what every control owes the hand

One contract, six pitches, no exceptions. These are the numbers the audit measured against; the pitches are judged by them, live, on this page.

§2Six pitches

PITCH I

The fitting — the door, as a piece of hardware

legiblediegetic
riason's study · brass, cut and stamped
sigil · the bench · chiselled, chipped at the edge
divination · felt, worn where the thumb goes
toybox · glazed tile, grout gone soft

the pitch

The exit stops being a glyph in a corner and becomes a fitting the maker installed: a plate of the room's substrate, cut to the room's sharp radius family, with the word physically in it — stamped into the brass, chiselled into the stone, cut into the tile. Not hoverable metaphor: a real <button>, 44px tall, whose label is the room's own verb (shut, step out, leave the table, out).

The focus mark is the fitting lighting from the room's declared lamp — a bevelled inner edge plus the plate's own key, never a generic ring. Hover and focus are the same lighting; the difference is only that focus is unmissable from across the room.

why it clears the floor, and the covenant

  • F1 ink — light letterform on dark metal in brass/stone/felt; dark letterform on pale glaze in the bathroom.
  • F2–F4 — 104×52; a word, always. The glyph is demoted to the fitting's own stamped mark, if a room wants one at all.
  • F7 not a library — the plate changes material, radius, edge damage and light per room. What is shared is only that it is a fitting on the machine.
  • the tells — the chipped edge on sigil's plate, the worn thumb dish on the felt, the soft grout line on the tile: each is that traveller handling their own door.
PITCH II

The legend — help as a plate on the moulding

legiblediegetic
LEGEND · RIASON'S BOOKREV. C
riason states it flatly, on a part number
LEGEND · THE STONE REMEMBERS BEING TOUCHEDREV. A
sigil writes it like an inscription

the pitch

The ? moves off the screen's air and onto the machine's body. A screen-printed legend strip on the bezel moulding, worded by the room's own maker, set at a legible ink, opening the room's argument as a paper slip the traveller could have left in there. The help text stops being a modal about the UI and becomes the note the maker taped inside the case — which is what the covenant's anti-prose rule actually wants: text that is already in the world.

Wording stays in register: LEGEND · RIASON'S BOOK is a plate. How this works is a designer.

why it clears the floor

  • F1 — printed legend ink on the moulding clears body contrast; the slip button inverts to its own ink so it reads as a stamped control.
  • F2–F3 — the slip control is a 44px target with an inverted focus state, not a naked glyph.
  • F7 — the moulding exists on every machine, but its legend is cut in that room's material: brass-etched in the study, painted in the glasshouse, chalked in the observatory.
  • why the bezel — the audit's real complaint was that help/exit float over the room's art, at the room's expense. The moulding is the one surface that belongs to the machine everywhere without covering anyone's work.
PITCH III

The tab — one anatomy, every action

legiblediegetic
paper · cut, typed, hole punched
slate · engraved, salt-bloomed
powder · pressed, soft edges

the pitch

Today the de-facto button everywhere is a 50px pill — 4 to 20 per room — which is exactly the shared component the covenant bans. The tab is the replacement, and it is deliberately the oldest control in the machine: a cut index tab, a punched hole, the label typed or written by the maker, the action stated in a verb phrase the room would actually use.

What is shared is only anatomy: a ≥44px tab, a hole that shows it was threaded, a hole that proves it was made by a hand, and a focus state that reads as a press impression — the surface dips and the label sinks half a pixel, because that is what happens when you press something real. No glow, no ring, no pill.

The tab is also where F4 earns its keep: keep to the book, let the water take it, pour — a pill saying "Save" tells you nothing about where you are.

why it clears the floor

  • F1 — every tab is a dark-ink-on-pale or pale-on-dark pairing chosen for ≥7:1, never a mid-tone on a mid-tone.
  • F2 — 44px minimum height by construction; the tab's own cut edge gives it a clear neighbour gap.
  • F5 — tabs wrap at 439 instead of squeezing; the label never truncates to a sliver, because the tab is sized to its label.
  • F7 — three tabs above: paper, slate, powder. Same anatomy, three makings, no shared radius.
  • the drawn-prop ladder — silhouette (the tab's cut edge), form (the punched hole), detail (the seam where it was typed). Two radius families per room, one used here.
PITCH IV

The interlock — for the heavy things

legiblediegeticconsent
RELEASE · WATER
springs shut in 6s if you change your mind
rest — the guard is down
RELEASE · WATER
armed · one more press and the valve turns
armed — red, and it says so

the pitch

The covenant says a room that holds something heavy keeps its heaviness — do not soften it to make the room safe. It also says the exit and every reversal must be real, and that nothing a tap can destroy should be one tap away. The interlock is how both are true at once: the heavy control wears a hinged guard, and the guard is the honest statement that this one costs something.

Press once and the guard lifts and turns red — armed, and says it is armed. Press again and the valve turns. Walk away and the guard springs back shut in six seconds without sulking. It is the machine's version of a switch you cannot bump.

why it clears the floor

  • F1 — the guard's word is light on dark metal, the armed word lighter still.
  • F2 — both the guard and the throw are 44px; the guard is the bigger target while it is down, so the dangerous state has the smallest hit area, not the largest.
  • F4 — no glyph. The button says lift the guard and then let it go: the state is in the words, so it survives a screen reader, a colour-blind traveller and a 439px window.
  • F6 — the six-second fallback is a state change; under reduced motion the guard snaps rather than swings.
  • covenant §power and exit — the room still names its power relation, but now the naming is a piece of hardware the visitor can read before they lean on it.
PITCH V

The platen — typing as paper

legiblediegetic
say it to the tube
the sidecar's platen · the caret is a printed tick

the pitch

The machines you talk to have a 20px-tall input and a grey placeholder at 2.9:1. The platen makes the typing surface a rail of ruled paper at 44px, with the caret printed rather than a blinking system bar, the field's own rules faintly ruled through it, and return as a carriage return on a brass arm instead of a paper plane.

The wax disc at the right is the seal — the same ceremony the sidecar and the buddy already have. It is not a "send" button with a stamp decoration: it is the stamp, and the act of sending is pressing your seal into the page.

why it clears the floor

  • F1 — the ink is the ink the sidecar's feed already proved legible: dark matrix on ruled cream. The weakest thing in this pitch is the carriage return's ink.
  • F2 — the rail is 44px; the carriage return is 44px; the wax is 22px but is decorative and never the only control.
  • F5 — the rail is fluid and the send button wraps under it at 439 rather than crushing the line.
  • F6 — the printed caret stops blinking under reduced motion and simply sits there, which is a true state, not a missing one.
  • diegetic, not decorative — the paper is the feed paper the sidecar already prints on. Nothing new is invented; the input is finally made of the same stuff as the output.
PITCH VI

The haunt — the chrome remembers your hand

haunted computerlegiblediegetic
ASLEEP
at rest · dim to embers, not to invisibility (ink still ≥7:1)
nothing has your attention here
AWAKE · RIAson speaks
riason, once, politely: “the clasps noticed. they always notice.”
focused · the fitting wakes, the traveller says one clause
focus summons the room's voice
REMEMBERED
0.8s after you leave: the afterimage fades, the thumbprint stays
the machine's memory, made visible

the pitch

This is the pitch that makes the chrome haunted rather than merely well-made, and it is the one that answers the brief most directly — a computer that exists within the mind does not have a toolbar; it has things it remembers about your attention.

  • Focus is possession. When a control takes focus it wakes from embers to full light, and the room's own traveller says one clause — through the existing whisper channel, aria-live="polite", never a hover tooltip. The machine is not labelling itself; it is noticing you.
  • Attention leaves a mark. The focus outline lingers as a spectral afterimage for 0.8s after you move on, then fades — and the last thing you touched keeps a faint thumbprint (a lifted patina ellipse) until you next come round.
  • Rest is dim, never hidden. Unfocused chrome drops to ember — but the ember ink is still ≥7:1, because a control you can't read is a control that isn't there. Legibility is not a mood.

Under prefers-reduced-motion the afterimage does not animate: it appears at its final low opacity and holds, and the whisper prints as static text. The function survives, the flourish does not.

why it clears the floor

  • F3 — this pitch is the fix for the four rooms that currently ship 32px bare glyphs with no focus state at all.
  • F1 — ember and awake are both measured on the plate, and the ember is the one worth arguing about: it is the floor, not a shade below it.
  • F6 — every animation in this pitch has a named instant equivalent: linger → static low-opacity duplicate, whisper → printed line, wake → jump to lit.
  • the mind, not a theme — the covenant already says the room remembers. This makes the chrome remember too, so the machine's memory is not only in the room behind the CRT but in the hand that keeps coming back to the same three buttons.
  • not a library — the traveller's voice, the whisper's wording and the thumbprint's material are per-room. Only the mechanism is shared.

THE ARGUMENT I EXPECTCovenant §2 says no shared component library and no consistent button style across rooms. This pitch shares size, ink, word and focus — and nothing else. The defence is that those four are not style: they are the difference between a fitting and a decoration. A door is a door in every house. The hinges, the timber, the paint and the wear are the room's. If a reviewer wants the machine to keep shipping a 22px unreadable carving as a "room's personality", that personality is a bug wearing a costume.
WHAT THIS BUYS THE COVENANTEvery fitting here is a chance for a tell: the chipped plate, the worn thumb dish, the punched hole, the thumbprint. The generic pill gives a traveller nothing to make. Hardware gives them edges to chip, habits to reveal, and a part number to stamp.

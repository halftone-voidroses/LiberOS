The Second Sweeping

Eight rooms and two shell jobs, revisited from inside the covenant — every surface read as its traveller would have made it, not as a screen that holds features. The plan keeps what works, retires what decorates, and gives each room one honest material.

Dreams — the reading desk, rebuilt

›
what sits there now A journal form, a ledger list, and a reading that slides over everything — the interpretation is one wide paper sheet, visually separate from the dream it reads, with associations and actions stacked below.
what gets built
  • The desk becomes a spread. Dream on the left as written text set in a soft-edged "cloud" plate (paper with deckled edge, the fog behind it); the reading on the right in the workbook hand, the two joined by one red thread per quoted association — the threads already exist, they just gain a two-column reason to.
  • The develop beat stays and plays across the spread: the left sheet develops, then the right page's ink fades in — one motion, gated by prefers-reduced-motion.
  • Marginalia keeps its place on the dream sheet's back; keep / plant / attach become brass fittings on the spread's spine, not buttons floating under.
  • The journal form gets covenant material: the entry paper sits on the desk's blotter; the ledger below becomes a card drawer, one row per dream, the same row grammar the satchel ledger uses.
  • Toy-box level finish: named light (the reading lamp upper-left), contact shadows under both sheets, one specular pass on the cloud plate, silhouette-first at 64px, detail budget held — the fog is the room's air, not a texture.

III

Liberchat — the second tube

›
what sits there now Chat lives behind a small lamp button in the room's corner; the panel opens as a floating card. The desktop also carries a separate chat entry — two doors to one room.
what gets built
  • A dot-matrix sidecar CRT on the desk, right of the main machine: smaller bezel, amber-phosphor panel, scanline bloom at the machine's --crt-intensity, visible stand and rear cable into the dark — a real object on the desk, not a card.
  • The dot-matrix grammar: messages print as perforated feed paper — sprocket holes down both margins, monospace matrix type, the courier's name in the feed's margin stamp. New lines feed upward; the tearing edge is where you type.
  • Wired to the same chat engine — liberchat's transport, presence and history are untouched; only the surface changes. The lamp button retires; the sidecar itself is the door (toggle, keyboard shortcut, one aria name).
  • The main CRT shifts right a measured amount to make desk room; the room behind (corkboard, shelf, window) re-balances its composition to keep the spread honest at 1280 and at 439.
  • Covenant: two named lights now — the lamp and the two tubes; the sidecar catches the lamp warm on its left flank and throws its own faint amber onto the desk it stands on. Contact shadow mandatory.

IV

Sea — one voice for the water

›
what sits there now The room mixes registers: instrument labels ("weigh", counters, "breath phase: in/out") speak in UI, while the pitch's sea speaks in weather. The two voices argue and the water loses.
what gets built
  • One diegetic voice pass: every control and label re-set as the instrumentary of a shore station — brass plates, stamped words, no UI verbs. "weigh" becomes the scale's own engraving; the breath toggle becomes a small brass valve with an engraved IN · OUT arc.
  • Copy sweep: the tour, the help, the placeholders and the empty states rewritten in the sea's weather-voice; nothing becomes poetic mush — the covenant's anti-prose rule holds: the maker stamps the plate, the sea moves the water.
  • Visual congruence: panel edges adopt the brass-and-water material the tide clock already owns; the waterline ledger and the release rite inherit it, so every element sits in one material family.

X

Buddy — the stone that speaks in carvings

›
what sits there now The buddy answers in chat-styled transcript lines inside the room — the voice of a messenger app bolted to a standing stone.
what gets built
  • Inscriptions, not messages. The buddy's replies are cut into the stone's face: a line of carving that surfaces glyph by glyph, holds while read, then weathers back into the rock. Nothing accumulates — the stone was never a log.
  • Little signs accompany speech: the stone's kept-marks (the carved symbols SYSTEM 04 already renders) glow softly in turn as emphasis — the stone gestures with its own history.
  • Your input stays a quiet offering: the text field becomes a shallow brass dish at the stone's base; what you send appears briefly as an embossed line on the stone's flank, then settles.
  • History keeps its door: the full transcript remains available through the satchel/book where it already persists — the room shows the moment, the book keeps the record.
  • Accessibility: carved lines are real text (aria-live), the fading is visual only, reduced-motion shows the line and holds it until dismiss.

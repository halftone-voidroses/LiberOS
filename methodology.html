example cycle a traveller's thesis

  1. Pick a tool from the Games or Exercises section that appeals to you right now.
  2. Follow the instructions — don't overthink, just do the exercise.
  3. Notice what you feel, think or sense, and write down those words within the "Interpretation" section of the tool.
  4. Save your artifact to your satchel (click "Save locally" when available).
  5. Later, compare two artifacts together using the Satchel's relation menu to note how they connect.

For example: You love tarot and the archetypes associated with it. Perhaps the random two cards drawn are The Fool (the journey beginning) and The Magician (controlling the path forward). You save them to your satchel. That night, you have a dream about these cards, or a random thought while going for a walk. You rush home and under the Amplification page you draw the symbol that appears to you in your dream, and save it to your satchel. Over time, you can lay these on top of each other. This results in a symbol that represents not just these two things, but the connection between them.

There are lots of tools that generate random results. There are no specific ways to use these; the purpose is to become aware of your own inner rhythm, not achieving a specific result.

amplification a traveller's thesis

Creating a sigil and infusing it with a cohort (step‑by‑step):

  1. Set an intention — write it in the box below and save it to your satchel (for example, "I want to see what's behind my fear of conflict").
  2. Let your hand move — on the canvas, draw freely without planning. If you are struggling to come up with ideas, click the Inspiration button to generate words.
  3. Notice the feelings — if you feel restless, bored or emotional, that's part of the information. Keep drawing until you reach a natural stopping point.
  4. When you feel finished, look at the image. What stands out? What does it remind you of? Write down three observations in the Interpretation box below.
  5. Save the sigil to your satchel (using the "Save locally" button) and optionally save your interpretation notes separately.
